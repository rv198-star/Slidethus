# Restrained photographic editorial contrast

Consider when material, place, people or craft imagery supports a refined tone, alongside substantial analytical evidence. It is a visual grammar, not a rule for a particular industry.

## Borrowable grammar

- Relate desaturated imagery, deep black, light neutrals and narrow copper/warm-metal accents. Example anchors: #252525, #A6A6A6 and #9D5A37. Richness comes from tonal depth, crop, scale and type rather than gold decoration everywhere.
- Pair spacious image-led propositions with denser chart/interpretation compositions. Carry the same title roles, alignment, line weight and color meaning into both.
- Use restrained serif/sans-serif contrast when supported by readable local fonts. Keep chart type quiet and consistent with the body.
- A clear text band can separate a claim from an image, but it should serve legibility rather than become generic glass-card styling. Give photography a planned crop and meaningful subject.

## Adapt, do not inherit

Reject the source's all-white data-page rule, universal narrative-photo requirement, small chart-label sizes, fixed sidebar count and financial appendix furniture. Data pages can retain atmospheric depth through coherent tonal or field treatment without compromising chart contrast. Missing production images cannot become blank placeholders. The word “luxury” does not certify quality: compare difficult analytical pages against the image-led direction, not only the cover.
