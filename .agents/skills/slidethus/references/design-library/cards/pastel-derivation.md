# Progressive explanation with stable visual anchors

Consider when a mechanism, transformation or sequence is easier to understand by revealing changes around a stable diagram. It does not require photography, but it does require correct, explainable relationships.

## Borrowable grammar

- Keep a base diagram and viewpoint stable across related pages; emphasize the new condition, operation or result instead of redrawing unrelated layouts. Sequence length follows the argument.
- Link diagram elements and their textual/equation counterparts by consistent semantic color. Example anchors: readable ink #172C34, blue #0085FD and magenta #C32E8F, with almond #FCEEDC as an optional local explanation field.
- Arrange starting condition, transformation and consequence in a readable path; use a centered mechanism where a horizontal flow would misrepresent it.
- Neutral sans-serif explanations and legible mathematical notation serve different roles. A gentle chapter treatment can provide a pause without turning all evidence pages into low-contrast pastels.

## Adapt, do not inherit

Discard mandatory academic section order, forced equations/ablations, fixed panel counts and a blanket dark-page ban. Do not add proof steps or data that the material lacks. Static progressive slides are sufficient; no animation requirement follows. Complex equations/vector diagrams require supported rendering or an explicitly manifested asset, not an invented native capability. For a one-page synthesis, a comparative chart or compact explanation may be better than a forced sequence.
