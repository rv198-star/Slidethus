# Editorial columns with chapter-color rhythm

Consider when several substantive explanations and strong images need to coexist in an editorial presentation. Useful for medium–high density with expressive section changes; printed-page copy lengths need adaptation for live viewing.

## Borrowable grammar

- Build column relationships from a common grid, switching between a wider image and narrower explanation, a broader text area and smaller image, or aligned evidence columns as content requires.
- Warm gray/black/white provide continuity; choose related chapter accents deliberately rather than making a rainbow of unrelated pages. Example anchors: #E5E3DD, #000000 and #FFD200.
- Use weight and scale to distinguish claims, pull quotes and evidence. Place long text on a calm opaque surface, not a busy photo.
- Crop or texture imagery consistently. Chapter and case pages can differ in intensity while sharing type, alignment and image treatment. Dense and spacious pages should both advance the narrative.

## Adapt, do not inherit

Discard fixed thirds, edge-navigation furniture, mandatory photo counts, prescribed long word counts and the prohibition on numeric charts. A quantitative relationship may need a real chart even though the source mostly uses data sentences. Do not add halftone texture everywhere or use a multicolor cover to justify incoherent body palettes. If genuine imagery is unavailable, use an honest evidence carrier or choose a less image-dependent direction; never label generic imagery as case evidence.
