# Evidence with a judgment rail

Consider when a central chart, comparison or matrix needs a clearly separated interpretation, at medium–high density. Less useful for a short emotional statement or a single small fact.

## Borrowable grammar

- Establish a reading path from assertion and scope to main evidence, then interpretation. A broad evidence area with a narrower side rail can clarify this relationship; alternate with full-width charts or framework pages when their structure needs room.
- Start with white/cool-gray surfaces, deep navy structure and a blue focus. Example anchors: #041C2C and #2151FF. Use neutral steps for supporting series; labels and contrast, not extra colors, distinguish hierarchy.
- A serif title against sans-serif data gives editorial contrast if available fonts remain legible. Retain the same type roles on dense pages and transitions.
- Direct labels, forecast distinction and selective callouts can connect evidence to the takeaway. Data must determine chart choice; a plot or matrix is not a decoration.

## Adapt, do not inherit

Discard the source's mandatory chart/table on every page, universal photo ban, fixed matrix sizes, numbered-navigation furniture and no-large-whitespace rule. Do not ban required confidentiality labels or add institutional marks. Exact side-rail ratios belong to Layout Plans, not a style command. Avoid repeating the rail when it merely divides scarce space; dense source typography is not a projection font standard.
