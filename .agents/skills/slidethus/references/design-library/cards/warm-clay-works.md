# Warm typography and tactile accents

Consider when a warm, crafted tone with low–medium density suits the communication, especially when type, comparisons and short process explanations can do substantial work. Do not select merely because a topic sounds artisanal.

## Borrowable grammar

- Relate warm off-white, ink-brown text and terracotta emphasis rather than choosing unrelated warm colors. Example anchors: #F9F4E9, #5C4630 and #C0651F; blush #F2DCD4 can support a restrained secondary field.
- Pair an expressive but readable title face with quieter body/data typography. Preserve functional roles with available fonts; decorative CJK lettering is not needed to mimic an exact source font.
- Use thin dividers to separate a statistic rail, comparison or process explanation. Space should group information, not create empty content slots.
- A limited tactile motif or offset image backing may provide continuity. Adapt surface depth and image placement across the deck instead of repeating the same corner decoration.

## Adapt, do not inherit

The source's all-off-white backgrounds, body-photo ban, chart-type bans, one-highlight count and fixed corner ornaments are not portable rules. This reference can become too quiet: use material, imagery, considered color fields or typographic scale where the content warrants them, without abandoning the warm relationships. Retain needed charts and readable labels; do not force every metric into a target-versus-actual business report.
