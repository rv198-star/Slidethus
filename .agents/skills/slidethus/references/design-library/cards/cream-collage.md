# Bold geometric editorial fields

Consider when short propositions, simple comparisons or timelines need energetic typographic presence without relying on photography. Less suitable as the sole grammar for detailed evidence tables.

## Borrowable grammar

- Cream and ink provide a base for a red/blue relationship: example anchors #F3E8D4, #171717, #F05A39 and #2657D7. Give the accent roles clear priority rather than distributing equal saturation everywhere.
- Large sans-serif titles and numbers can balance hard-edged fields, diagonal anchors and open space. Field geometry should organize a message, not become a generic wall of cards.
- Use a timeline, proportion comparison or number-led composition when the available facts support it. Vary scale and grouping across content roles while retaining the same edge and type language.
- A stronger closing or transition surface can punctuate quieter evidence pages. Preserve readable labels, units and sources within the expressive system.

## Adapt, do not inherit

The source was a six-slide sample: its exact node counts, three-path panels, green CTA button and one-chart cap are not universal. Do not import web interaction or add a CTA the narrative does not call for. Fixed red/blue fields must not override a real brand palette or meaningful positive/negative semantics. Pictures and gradients are neither required nor globally forbidden; choose treatments coherently and plan their roles.
