# Documentary image and interpretation

Consider when real contextual imagery can carry a substantial part of the message, paired with a concise judgment and supporting evidence. Poor fit for a photo-led direction when suitable assets are unavailable.

## Borrowable grammar

- Give one meaningful scene visual dominance and put its interpretation in a distinct text zone. Vary image/text splits, image-led transitions and chart explanations while maintaining alignment and crop logic.
- A pale base, forest-green structure and a vivid green focus can connect photography and data. Example anchors: #002A12 and #1ECF77. Use darker body text where pale source colors fail contrast.
- Neutral sans-serif type and clear title-to-body scale allow imagery to remain the focal point. Keep units attached to large numerals without shrinking them beyond readability.
- Use foreground color for the current subject and quieter related values for comparisons. A chart-led page can retain image-page typography and spacing without needing a decorative photograph.

## Adapt, do not inherit

Discard the observed 23/38 photographic-page ratio, single-dark-page cap, fixed photo count and mandatory target/actual/attainment story. Do not make every page white or every sentence pale gray. A photo is contextual evidence only when its source and relationship support the claim. Generated illustration may be used when honestly appropriate, but must not masquerade as documentary proof. Reserve image regions before production; absent assets require sourcing or explicit replanning.
